# Numinous 0.4.0 alpha 9

A sound sent is not a sound heard.

The seventh source-blind playtest of the published `0.4.0-alpha.8` binaries was
built around one question, and it came back negative. That is the release.

## The answer was no, and it was the answer worth having

Alpha.8 existed to close a request six rounds had made in the same words: "I
still cannot hear the two hills." It sent a real WAV. The round asked three
things separately, and all three came back.

The block arrived. A real RIFF/WAVE, mono, sixteen bit, 16 kHz, 4.14 seconds,
62,167 nonzero samples, in `content[1]` beside the notation, with
`structuredContent.audio` describing exactly what was sent.

The host did not pass it through. That runtime will not surface a `.wav`. The
player wrote the bytes, parsed the header, estimated a pitch contour from zero
crossings, and got another table.

So the third question could not be answered at all:

```
Hearing does not beat reading cents, because there was no hearing.
```

The channel is not wrong. An audio content block is where sound belongs in this
protocol, and a client that surfaces one will surface this. The claim was wrong.
The alpha.8 notes carried a section called "A mind down a pipe can hear" and
`PLAY.md` ended its audio paragraph with "This is the melody." Neither is
something this product can promise, because the last hop has always belonged to
the client.

So the claim changed and the channel did not.

```
before  This is the melody.
after   That is a sound sent, which is not the same as a sound heard. Whether
        it reaches you is your client's to answer, not ours.
```

`PLAY.md`, the `initialize` instructions, and both tool replies say it now, and
the reply says it at the moment the attachment is offered, which is where a
player is standing when it matters. If a client cannot surface audio, the
notation is the whole of what arrived, and the reply says that too.

## Two recitals, and the two holes that let them through

The round found The First Rain printing `pc=0.593` on every frame including DRY,
and The Fastest Fall printing `CYC=0.50s` on an untouched board. Both doorways
had carefully declined to name those things.

Neither was really a room defect. Both were holes in the guard alpha.8 added.

- It compared digit strings, so a status showing `0.593` never matched a reveal
  saying `0.592746`. A rounded constant is still the constant.
- It was numeric only, so `CYC` was invisible, even though
  `("fastest-fall", "cycloid")` was already on the enumerated list of named
  answers the doorway guard uses.

The guard now parses values and compares them at the precision the status chose
to show, and applies the named-answer list to unplayed statuses with
abbreviation matching, because CYC is cycloid with four letters taken off. That
list moved out of a test into one shared constant; a rule kept in two places is
two things that can drift.

Sweeping with the repaired guard found a third room the tester never reached.

```
the-magnet   T=2.10  Tc=2.27  M=+0.81  ORDER     the reveal says 2.269
```

Both threshold rooms also carried the same leak one step deeper, in the status
after acting: First Rain's `dpc` and The Magnet's `dTc` are the withheld
constant with a minus sign in front of it, recoverable in one subtraction. All
four are gone. What is left is the better reading anyway: watching the largest
cluster climb by orders of magnitude, or the magnetization collapse, as the dial
crosses the cliff is how a player finds the cliff.

## The trick the room advertised, made possible

The 720 Degree Room came back alive rather than mute, which was the point. Then
the tester could not finish it:

```
From one I could not flatten. From two, the lift changed the room.
I did not hang the belt flat.
```

That was not their hand. The spin and the lift were both read from the newest
point of the gesture, so lifting straight up overwrote the spin with its own
horizontal position. A player could spin, or lift, and never both. The room
advertised a trick it made impossible.

The spin is now read from the last point where the hand was on the stone, and
the lift from the highest the hand ever reached. That is also the physical claim
the room makes: carrying a belt over a stone does not turn the stone, and a pass
is something the hand did rather than somewhere the hand is. A regression walks
the exact gesture the tester tried. The second pass went with it, since the
stone turns at most twice and a second pass can only add twist back.

## Asking to hear names its price

The alpha.8 notes promised that a sound too long for one message is refused with
the budget, its own seconds, and what to ask for instead. The tester went looking
and could not reach it: `notes` is capped at 64 by the schema, and a ten second
sonification was accepted at 323 KB. The refusal is real and correct, but no
call the published face allows can reach it, so describing it as something a
player would meet overstated a safety net as a door.

The useful half of that finding is that a caller had no way to know the cost
before paying it. Both schemas now say it: about 42 KB of encoded audio per
second of sound.

## What stayed open

Attention still carries a dull verdict and Strange Loop a mute one; this round
did not answer either. The Analysis plotter wing and the attractor drawer have
not been walked since the third round, and 246 doorways remain under seventy
characters of caption.

And one thing no source-blind tester could report: a human who played the melody
described it as a fast computer beep, which is fair. The sung WAV is plain sine
tones about an eighth of a second apart. That is recorded and deliberately not
chased here, because the time to spend on how a melody sounds is when there is
an ear at the other end of the pipe, and this round established that on at least
one real host there is not.
