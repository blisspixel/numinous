## The mathematical question

Blockwise Alperin equates Brauer-character counts with conjugacy classes of weights for each finite-group block.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Group-action orbits and paired count diagrams are visual; character values may be explicitly mapped to sound.

## What makes it hard

The theorem is numerical equality, not a canonical bijection. A visual pairing must not imply one unless constructed, and modular representation calculations need specialist exact infrastructure beyond ordinary permutation groups.

## Inside the construction

### finite-object

The count uses ordered tuples (x,y,u_1,...,u_n) satisfying a commutator-product relation, generating J, and having a prescribed multiset of p-singular conjugacy classes.

- **Source locator:** eq:tuple-relation
- **Numinous use:** Enumerate short tuples in a small group and filter them by each condition.
- **Limit:** The u_i remain ordered; prescribing a multiset does not quotient by their permutations.

### invariant

The quotient is by simultaneous conjugation, and the theorem asserts eventual p^h divisibility uniformly over the prescribed multisets when O_p(J)=1.

- **Source locator:** thm:tuple-divisibility
- **Numinous use:** Compare orbit counts and p-adic valuations rather than counting raw tuples.
- **Limit:** The statement gives no practical threshold through this selected reading.
