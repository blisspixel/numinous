## The mathematical question

Finite cylinders with triangular perpendicular bases allegedly cover a regular tetrahedron below a natural half-projection-area benchmark.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Interlocking transparent prisms reveal a spatial surprise; overlap-triggered tones are mapped, not acoustic resonances.

## What makes it hard

The actual theorem takes epsilon at most 1/2000 and m = 2 ceil(2/epsilon^2), requiring at least sixteen million cylinders. The strict area saving is tiny; a simple rendered cover would illustrate the idea, not instantiate this certified construction.
