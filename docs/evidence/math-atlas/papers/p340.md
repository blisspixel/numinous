## The mathematical question

A fixed ordered binary matrix allegedly defeats polynomial removal bounds when copies preserve row and column order and match zeros too.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Binary tapestries and highlighted submatrices create pattern play; rows may map to rhythms.

## What makes it hard

The sixty-six-by-sixty-six obstruction is large for exhaustive search. Matching zeros as well as ones and permitting either flip direction are essential; ordinary graph-submatrix containment is not equivalent.

## Inside the construction

### construction

A mostly off-diagonal 64-by-64 anchor contains all five-bit words in a designated subblock, then two rows and columns attach a fixed body.

- **Source locator:** eq:pattern-definition
- **Numinous use:** Build the exact pattern and highlight its identifying bit signatures.
- **Limit:** Rows and columns are ordered; arbitrary permutations change the problem.

### representation

The larger matrices organize row and column blocks along a binary tree with singleton leaves.

- **Source locator:** sec:construction
- **Numinous use:** Zoom through the tree-indexed matrix.
- **Limit:** The distance and copy-count lower bounds require later pinning arguments.
