## The mathematical question

The lex-plus-powers Betti theorem claims a monomial comparison ideal preserves the Hilbert function and dominates every graded Betti number.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Monomial staircases and Betti grids connect shape and relations; graded counts may map to tones.

## What makes it hard

Characteristic zero and regular-sequence hypotheses matter. Equal Hilbert functions alone do not imply the asserted Betti ordering; supported exact computations should stand independently of the general theorem claim.

## Inside the construction

### algorithm

For a degree-(d+1) vector, subtracting from its last nonzero coordinate gives the lex-largest predecessor; this order-preserving map proves that a lex segment's bounded shadow is lex.

- **Source locator:** box:three-inequalities
- **Numinous use:** Compute shadows by predecessor tests and animate monomial movement in a bounded box.
- **Limit:** Coordinates have individual capacity bounds, in the given order.

### certificate

For stable X, lex replacement preserves size while decreasing shadow size, decreasing the zero-slice count and increasing the top-slice count.

- **Source locator:** box:three-inequalities
- **Numinous use:** Compare the three exact counters as slices are compressed.
- **Limit:** The three inequalities use stability; arbitrary subsets need not satisfy the same statement.
