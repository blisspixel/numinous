## The mathematical question

The dyadic triangular form claims an explicit bound on the sum of absolute local contributions across arbitrary finite scales.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Visual: editable input fields and linked operator-response maps with scale controls. Optional response-to-pitch audio is sonification, not the operator itself sounding.

## What makes it hard

The dyadic model is not the continuous transform. Unrestricted real inputs and independently varying bounded coefficients must be retained in the implementation.

## Inside the construction

### finite-identity

Pairwise input arrays become weighted edge matrices; their cyclic trace with Haar signs equals the normalized local integral.

- **Source locator:** eq:weighted-edge; eq:trace-integral
- **Numinous use:** Switch between a colored three-array pattern, its matrices and an exact finite sum.
- **Limit:** Probability weights and the scale prefactor are essential; an unweighted matrix trace represents a different quantity.

### combinatorial-mechanism

The admissible dyadic children have parity b0 xor b1 xor b2=0. Their signs are pairwise independent but jointly constrained, and the averaged Gram energy controls the local contribution.

- **Source locator:** prop:increase; eq:child-gram
- **Numinous use:** A splitting puzzle can expose pairwise independence, parity and an energy ledger on one finite object.
- **Limit:** Numerical energy checks illustrate the local proposition, not the all-scale singular-integral theorem.
