## The mathematical question

Smooth surfaces admit the reported isometric immersion into four dimensions.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Proposed visual: Bend a metric mesh while tracking edge lengths and rotate its four-dimensional projection. No physical sound model is claimed.

## What makes it hard

A projected image does not preserve intrinsic lengths; a mesh fit does not certify a smooth isometric immersion.

## Inside the construction

### construction

The oscillation plane is perpendicular to both G_y and G_yy, removing a first error in the yy metric coefficient while retaining a two-dimensional velocity circle.

- **Source locator:** lem:velocity-plane
- **Numinous use:** A jet editor can calculate the plane and display tangent, normal and curvature directions in linked projections.
- **Limit:** The independence and collar conditions are essential; an arbitrary surface patch may not satisfy them.

### calculation

Replacing projected velocity v by a circle of radius sqrt(abs(v)^2+a^2) gives the leading rank-one metric gain.

- **Source locator:** eq:leading-velocity
- **Numinous use:** Let users vary amplitude and directly measure the induced metric defect.
- **Limit:** Leading oscillations are not the exact smooth immersion; correctors and convergence remain necessary.
