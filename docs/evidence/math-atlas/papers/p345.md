## The mathematical question

A three-dimensional complete normal local domain allegedly admits no finite nonzero maximal Cohen-Macaulay module.

## Visual and sonic possibility

A resolution or monomial diagram is possible; sound would be mapped and optional.

## What makes it hard

Failure to find a module in a finite search cannot prove nonexistence. No faithful direct play mechanic is identified; the complete local object and its dimension should not become a misleading surface picture.

## Inside the construction

### representation

Blowing up four general-position points in the plane gives an intersection lattice with basis ell,e_1,...,e_4 and diagonal pairing 1,-1,-1,-1,-1.

- **Source locator:** sec:surface
- **Numinous use:** Make line and exceptional-divisor intersections editable exact integer calculations.
- **Limit:** A lattice computation alone does not construct the branched cover or local domain.

### construction

The six strict transforms of joining lines and four exceptional curves form ten rational boundary components with fifteen nodes; each component meets three others.

- **Source locator:** sec:surface
- **Numinous use:** Toggle blowups and trace how incident plane lines become disjoint while opposite lines still intersect.
- **Limit:** General-position hypotheses control this incidence pattern.
