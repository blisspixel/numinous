## The mathematical question

Cell-mass derivatives form a weighted graph Laplacian with constants as kernel; a weighted-zero normalization fixes the unique intercept vector for prescribed positive masses.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Proposed visual: Drag three target masses and watch the transport map change as a whole. No physical sound model is claimed.

## What makes it hard

The one-third exponent concerns dependence on the target measure in L2, not spatial Holder regularity of the map; finite fits cannot certify sharpness.

## Inside the construction

### algorithm

Cell-mass derivatives form a weighted graph Laplacian with constants as kernel; a weighted-zero normalization fixes the unique intercept vector for prescribed positive masses.

- **Source locator:** cells:laplacian; cells:weights
- **Numinous use:** Build a power-diagram mass solver with weighted graph-Laplacian Newton steps; export the same face-weight graph and normalized matrix to graph and matrix workbenches with geometry-derived edge semantics.
- **Limit:** Distinct sites and positive cell masses are required.

### example

On a cube, max(-x1,x1,a) and max(-x1,x1,a+(a/2)x2) preserve central mass but reassign a strip of source points; their gradients give explicit optimal maps.

- **Source locator:** sharpness:potentials; cells:optimality
- **Numinous use:** Make the three-plane tilt directly playable and calculate target displacement versus map change.
- **Limit:** The sharp exponent concerns the stated L2 map norm and Wasserstein target metric, not arbitrary transport distances.
