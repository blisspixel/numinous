## The mathematical question

Consecutive multiple ergodic averages allegedly converge pointwise for all lengths under mixing, without a convergence rate.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Multiple moving probes form linked ribbons; their actual sampled product can be audified.

## What makes it hard

Finite averages do not establish almost-everywhere convergence and no rate is promised. Use bounded observables and a specified invariant measure instead of assuming any chaotic-looking map satisfies the theorem.

## Inside the construction

### invariant

A low-rank projection captures only a bounded number of tensor products when both factor families have sparse large-overlap graphs.

- **Source locator:** thm:packing
- **Numinous use:** Rotate a testing subspace against a tensor family.
- **Limit:** The universal constant and asymptotic regime limit numerical sharpness.

### construction

Rectangular matrices are embedded into symmetric off-diagonal blocks so covariance trace and operator norm control Gaussian sums.

- **Source locator:** lem:packing-gaussian
- **Numinous use:** Compare rectangular sums with their symmetric dilations.
- **Limit:** The ergodic closure argument is outside this finite matrix mechanism.
