## The mathematical question

An isometry of unit spheres extends linearly in the reported setting.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Proposed visual: Reshape a norm ball and try to match distances on its boundary. No physical sound model is claimed.

## What makes it hard

A finite sample of preserved distances does not certify a sphere isometry.

## Inside the construction

### construction

A sphere isometry has only one possible positively homogeneous extension: T(x)=norm(x) f(x/norm(x)). Radial rays from an interior point meet the unit sphere uniquely.

- **Source locator:** lem:radial
- **Numinous use:** A norm-ball instrument can compute radial intersections and compare the induced extension on actual vectors.
- **Limit:** The asserted extension is real-linear; complex linearity is not claimed.

### diagnostic

The scalar interpolation defect is Lipschitz in its parameter and reverses sign under the inverse isometry; maximum attainment uses an ultrapower.

- **Source locator:** eq:defect; lem:attainment
- **Numinous use:** Plot defect sweeps for exact polygon norms and supplied sphere maps.
- **Limit:** Finite sampled zero defects do not prove the universal theorem, and the ultrapower is not a numerical algorithm.
