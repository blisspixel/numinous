## The mathematical question

A smooth metric can match Euclidean jets at a point yet resist a local three-dimensional immersion.

## Visual and sonic possibility

## What makes it hard

Failed numerical fitting cannot prove nonexistence; tiny deviations can be invisible at finite precision.

## Inside the construction

### limitation

The metric can share the Euclidean full Taylor jet at the origin while failing smooth realization on every neighborhood.

- **Source locator:** main.tex, introductory reference to cor:flat-jet
- **Numinous use:** Compare jets and neighborhood data as separate inspection tools.
- **Limit:** A finite jet or finite mesh cannot certify the nonrealizability.

### proof-mechanism

The pulse argument bounds admissible heights, shears coordinates by a rational slope approximation, and creates an oscillatory transverse mismatch between strip edges.

- **Source locator:** sec:pulse
- **Numinous use:** Visualize scalar Darboux compatibility and pulse residuals on a fixed patch.
- **Limit:** The Baire-category construction is not an explicit practical metric generator; both curvature signs occur.
