## The mathematical question

A product of two ten-dimensional simplices beats the twenty-dimensional simplex's normalized projection-body volume by an explicit rational ratio.

## Visual and sonic possibility

## What makes it hard

A low-dimensional rendering cannot show a twenty-dimensional body faithfully. The inequality concerns projection-body volume, not polar projection volume.

## Inside the construction

### finite-construction

A polytope's projection body is the Minkowski sum of segments plus or minus half a facet area times its unit normal.

- **Source locator:** lem:facets; eq:zonotope
- **Numinous use:** Build the projection body from visible face contributions and compare its support value with shadow area.
- **Limit:** Higher-dimensional facet volumes require exact or bounded arithmetic; visual shadows alone are not certificates.

### exact-counterexample

The normalized projection-body quantity is multiplicative under Cartesian products. Comparing two 10-simplices with a 20-simplex reduces to 22355476/22020096, which exceeds one.

- **Source locator:** eq:multiplicative; prop:simplex; sec:counterexample
- **Numinous use:** Search dimension pairs and export an integer inequality certificate; show lower-dimensional product shadows alongside the arithmetic.
- **Limit:** The decisive comparison is twenty-dimensional; a two-dimensional drawing must not be presented as the counterexample itself.
