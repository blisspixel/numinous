## The mathematical question

A group-ring matrix allegedly has Fuglede-Kadison determinant strictly between zero and one despite rational group-ring invertibility.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Spectral density and cumulative log-integral plots are plausible; spectral audification must be labeled approximate.

## What makes it hard

Finite determinants are not the infinite operator determinant. Near-zero spectrum dominates logarithmic integrals, so naive truncation is especially misleading; no faithful direct mechanic is established from the abstract.

## Inside the construction

### representation

Regular multiplication matrices embed a finite characteristic-two coefficient field into matrices over F_2, carrying group-ring products into block-matrix products.

- **Source locator:** lem:binary
- **Numinous use:** Expand a field coefficient into a binary multiplication matrix and verify product preservation.
- **Limit:** This does not turn the underlying infinite group into a finite group.

### invariant

A one-sided inverse XY=I yields a nonzero idempotent defect C=I-YX with XC=0 and CY=0.

- **Source locator:** eq:defect
- **Numinous use:** Track the defect through formal noncommutative multiplication and display which products vanish.
- **Limit:** Ordinary finite square matrices over a field cannot realize XY=I with YX!=I.
