## The mathematical question

The thin-tree theorem claims a spanning tree crossing every cut only a controlled fraction of the original edges.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Tree edges glow within a translucent host; cut-load tones are mapped.

## What makes it hard

The guarantee is simultaneous over all cuts, not merely cuts the user sampled. The universal constant is unspecified, and parallel edges count with multiplicity even when visually bundled.

## Inside the construction

### construction

Positive edge scalings followed by one invertible map normalize incidence vectors into an isotropic frame.

- **Source locator:** lem:auxiliary-representation
- **Numinous use:** Whiten a union of disjoint spanning trees.
- **Limit:** Strict positivity is needed to preserve every subset rank.

### certificate

A log-determinant potential is bounded below using the supplied tree bases and Cauchy-Binet.

- **Source locator:** eq:auxiliary-isotropy
- **Numinous use:** Compare leverage-like squared norms with the target 1/q.
- **Limit:** The existence proof is not yet a finite-bit implementation.
