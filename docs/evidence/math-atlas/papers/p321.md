## The mathematical question

The discrete-convexity claim controls sets outside unions of a universal number of likely family members under Bernoulli measure.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Subset mosaics and union layers show coverage; remaining measure maps to tone.

## What makes it hard

The theorem specifies k = 2^75 and measure at least 1-1/k, making literal union play an illustration rather than its useful regime. A finite sampled family cannot be assumed to meet this extremely strong hypothesis.

## Inside the construction

### construction

Select subsets lying in many hyperedges using degree d(I)=number of containing edges times rho^(r-|I|).

- **Source locator:** lem:covering
- **Numinous use:** Rank subsets by their weighted extension mass.
- **Limit:** Enumerating all subsets is only practical for small examples.

### identity

Summing d(I) rho^|I| over all subsets of size at most r equals 2^r times the family's total edge cost.

- **Source locator:** sec:covering
- **Numinous use:** Check the accounting behind the cover budget.
- **Limit:** Later pair sampling and the universal 2^75 constant remain separate.
