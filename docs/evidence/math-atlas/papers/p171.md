## The mathematical question

A convex body and its polar have an affine-invariant volume product, with recursively built Hanner bodies as claimed minimizers.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Visual: coupled geometric objects with exact coordinates and invariant readings. Optional invariant-to-pitch sonification is a chosen mapping, not inherent geometric sound.

## What makes it hard

The origin must stay interior and symmetry must be preserved. Low-dimensional exact examples do not validate the all-dimensional equality classification.

## Inside the construction

### convex-feasibility

A median of three points lies simultaneously on their pairwise metric intervals. Its three prescribed radii are half-sums of the pairwise distances.

- **Source locator:** eq:median-radii
- **Numinous use:** For a polygonal norm, intersect three translated scaled unit balls and expose exact feasibility and slack.
- **Limit:** A median need not exist or be unique for an arbitrary norm; the manuscript's existence conclusion uses its equality assumptions.

### geometric-reformulation

The proof encodes two bounded vectors with a fixed sum as an aggregate convex set, then tests membership after adding a scaled unit ball.

- **Source locator:** eq:aggregate-set; eq:median-membership
- **Numinous use:** Let the same draggable triangle show interval intersections and the equivalent convex membership question.
- **Limit:** A low-dimensional polygon calculation illustrates the reduction, not the general equality theorem.
