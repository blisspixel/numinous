## The mathematical question

The maximal triangular transform takes a supremum over both hard truncation endpoints, not just one shrinking radius.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Visual: editable input fields and linked operator-response maps with scale controls. Optional response-to-pitch audio is sonification, not the operator itself sounding.

## What makes it hard

A bounded endpoint grid misses continuum extrema and all-scale behavior. Unlike variation, the maximum does not measure repeated oscillation.

## Inside the construction

### matrix-calculus

The integral derivative formula for the sum of cubed singular values remains valid through rank changes, and each gradient entry is bounded by its row norm times column norm.

- **Source locator:** lem:rank-change; eq:entry-gradient
- **Numinous use:** Extend the matrix energy inspector to paths where singular values reach zero, with explicit row/column bounds.
- **Limit:** The supported second-derivative formula from companion work cannot simply be used across a changing kernel.

### event-accounting

Each matrix entry has its own on/off interval in scale and switches at most twice, enabling a ledger of absolute energy jumps.

- **Source locator:** prop:masked-dissipation
- **Numinous use:** Let players schedule matrix entries and watch both continuous energy evolution and discrete jumps.
- **Limit:** Allowing repeated arbitrary toggles would lose the paper's two-switch budget; output-dependent truncation is not arbitrary time control.
