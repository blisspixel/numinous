## The mathematical question

Planar Schrodinger evolution is claimed to recover endpoint H-one-third initial data almost everywhere with Gaussian regularization removed first.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Visual: linked source, spectrum, and evolving field views. Faithful playback only for an explicitly defined audible waveform; other norm-to-pitch mappings are sonification.

## What makes it hard

A finite spectral model automatically converges and cannot establish the endpoint theorem. Removing regularization before taking time to zero is substantive.

## Inside the construction

### diagnostic

The proof controls an error envelope formed by a supremum over time, rather than measuring error at a single selected frame.

- **Source locator:** eq:endpoint-error-envelope
- **Numinous use:** Keep a running time-window error view beside the animated field and compare spectral truncations on the same time grid.
- **Limit:** A sampled maximum is a lower bound on the continuous-time supremum unless additional derivative control is supplied.

### limit-order

Gaussian approximation is justified using uniform local mass plus an explicit rapidly decaying annular tail; its removal and the time limit are distinct operations.

- **Source locator:** lem:endpoint-heat-envelope; eq:endpoint-gaussian-tail
- **Numinous use:** Separate smoothing width, frequency cutoff and time controls so an experiment records which limit was varied first.
- **Limit:** Finite experiments do not prove the endpoint almost-everywhere statement or construct its common exceptional set.
