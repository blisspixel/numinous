## The mathematical question

A universal finite-field matrix substitution allegedly detects every nonzero bounded-size noncommutative formula, including characteristic two.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Matrix tiles expose noncommutative order; residue-to-tone mapping is optional.

## What makes it hard

Primality and formula-size promises are essential, while polynomial matrix dimension may still be prohibitive. A random tuple lacks the claimed universal guarantee and cannot be silently substituted.

## Inside the construction

### construction

Strictly lower triangular matrices encode word operators on z-jets, with entries (1+t)^(i(r-q-1))/(t^r-1).

- **Source locator:** eq:truncated-operators
- **Numinous use:** Multiply noncommuting words and inspect retained coefficients.
- **Limit:** The rightmost operator acts first.

### certificate

A proved order bound sets N=1+n binomial(2s,2); a second Taylor truncation replaces rational-function entries by prime-field matrices.

- **Source locator:** eq:operator-evaluation
- **Numinous use:** Track both truncation budgets explicitly.
- **Limit:** Detection depends on the formula-size bound and the denominator analysis.
