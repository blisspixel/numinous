## The mathematical question

Directed feedback vertex set breaks every directed cycle by deleting vertices; this manuscript claims constant-factor approximation hardness.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Cycle trails visibly stop after deletions; any cycle-length rhythm is a chosen mapping.

## What makes it hard

Acyclicity is readily certified after deletion, but optimality is harder. The universal hardness claim does not supply a solver or justify presenting heuristic deletion counts as minimum values.

## Inside the construction

### construction

For S original vertices, replace vertex v by ceil(S times its cost) independent clones and each arc by a complete bipartite connection.

- **Source locator:** lem:round-and-clone
- **Numinous use:** Expand weighted directed graphs into explicit unweighted ones.
- **Limit:** Positive rational costs and the stated cost bound control size.

### certificate

The optimum equals the minimum rounded weight of an original feedback vertex set: any surviving original cycle can select one surviving clone from each cluster.

- **Source locator:** eq:clone-exact-optimum
- **Numinous use:** Lift cycle witnesses and compare optimal deletions exhaustively.
- **Limit:** Partial cluster deletions do not improve the optimum; this is not an efficient optimization algorithm.
