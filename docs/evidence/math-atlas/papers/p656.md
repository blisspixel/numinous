Symplectic packing allows surprising high-dimensional capacity behavior.

## What makes it hard

Ordinary Euclidean packing is not symplectic packing, and the theorem starts above the planar case.

## Inside the construction

### decision-rule

For real dimension at least six, the claimed criterion combines total capacity-power volume with all pairwise capacity sums, both strict for closed sources inside an open target.

- **Source locator:** intro:inequalities
- **Numinous use:** A packing calculator can immediately evaluate both margins and show volume-feasible but pairwise-forbidden inputs.
- **Limit:** Passing the inequalities is not an explicit embedding, and the rule does not apply in dimension four.

### construction

The six-dimensional large-ball case uses a cubic base because a conic has genus zero; the quadric blowup has an explicit quadratic inverse map and intersection classes.

- **Source locator:** six:quadric
- **Numinous use:** Provide a moment-polytope and intersection-class construction viewer.
- **Limit:** A Euclidean ball arrangement does not visualize a symplectic embedding faithfully.
