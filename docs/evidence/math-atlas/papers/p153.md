## The mathematical question

Signed coefficient polynomials are claimed to have nearly constant complex modulus around the entire circle, with independently chosen signs at each length.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Visual: complex phasor path, sign tiles, and certified radial envelopes. Faithful signal playback of the chosen real waveform; complex modulus is a separate reading.

## What makes it hard

One fixed infinite sign stream need not have ultraflat prefixes. A dense FFT grid cannot certify uniform extrema, and real audio is not identical to complex modulus.

## Inside the construction

### finite-construction

Prescribed phases are retained while relaxed coefficient magnitudes are rounded through successive dyadic grids; simultaneous sign reversal keeps total defect mass from increasing.

- **Source locator:** lem:complex-rounding
- **Numinous use:** Let players snap a relaxed coefficient vector to two choices per phase and compare the resulting Fourier error.
- **Limit:** The discrepancy lemma supplies existence; a practical rounding strategy still needs an algorithm and runtime contract.

### sampling-certificate

The proof samples real and imaginary Fourier values on a 20n grid, then uses a derivative bound to control the whole circle from those samples.

- **Source locator:** eq:rounding-matrix-error
- **Numinous use:** Show sampled maxima alongside a justified enclosing bound, with exact input coefficients retained.
- **Limit:** A dense-looking plot alone is not a uniform flatness certificate, and the manuscript leaves absolute constants unspecified.
