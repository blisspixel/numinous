## The mathematical question

The lower-envelope refinement keeps modulus above one sixteenth of its natural scale while making the maximum nearly minimal.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Visual: complex phasor path, sign tiles, and certified radial envelopes. Faithful signal playback of the chosen real waveform; complex modulus is a separate reading.

## What makes it hard

A positive sampled minimum can miss a narrow zero. This result does not claim the minimum approaches the optimal scale, unlike the ultraflat companion.

## Inside the construction

### phase-design

A short low-modulus gap receives a phase-matched wave. Each gap owns a disjoint derivative interval, limiting which gaps can contribute stationary phase at the same frequency.

- **Source locator:** prop:correction
- **Numinous use:** Expose phase, envelope and instantaneous-frequency tracks together so a player repairs a dip without losing sight of the coefficient cost.
- **Limit:** The construction assumes the stated smooth input, endpoint phase and small-gap conditions; it is not a universal audio repair algorithm.

### rounding-boundary

Relaxed real coefficients are rounded with an error governed by their total defect mass; the cited partial-coloring input is used existentially.

- **Source locator:** lem:rounding-matrix
- **Numinous use:** Compare a relaxed design, an explicit bounded search for signs and both upper and lower modulus readings.
- **Limit:** An upper peak bound does not guarantee a positive minimum; large-N thresholds and constants are not interactive guarantees.
