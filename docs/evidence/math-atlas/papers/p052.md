## The mathematical question

Large normalized consecutive-prime gaps are claimed to occur with positive lower density for every fixed size threshold.

## What makes it hard

The positive proportion depends on the chosen threshold and is not a uniform constant. Finite troughs in running proportions do not disprove the asymptotic claim. Smooth support and simplex restrictions are part of the construction. The moment identities are asymptotic claims; finite divisor sums and finite kernel approximations do not verify them.

### exact-construction

Cumulative integration produces F_j whose complete mixed derivative is (-1)^j f_j; these derivatives vanish on coordinate faces although F_j itself need not.

### author-claim

The marked moment is expressed as a sum of squared norms of f_j+T f_(j+1), while the unmarked mass uses norms of f_j alone.
