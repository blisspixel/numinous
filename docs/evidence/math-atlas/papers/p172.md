## The mathematical question

For nonsymmetric bodies the polar is centered at the Santalo point, with simplices as claimed volume-product minimizers.

## Visual and sonic possibility

Visual: coupled geometric objects with exact coordinates and invariant readings. Optional invariant-to-pitch sonification is a chosen mapping, not inherent geometric sound.

## What makes it hard

The center is part of the problem and cannot be fixed at the centroid without justification. Numerical center optimization does not certify the general inequality.

## Inside the construction

### optimization-object

The polar-volume integral uses negative powers of support-function slack; it is strictly convex and diverges at the boundary, locating a unique interior center.

- **Source locator:** lem:cone-reduction
- **Numinous use:** Move the center of a convex polygon and inspect the exact polar polygon and its volume.
- **Limit:** Numerical optimization needs an explicit stopping bound; this alone does not establish a sharp volume-product inequality.

### duality-accounting

Passing to a cone pairs linear coordinate changes with inverse-transpose changes on its positive dual; the Laplace product retains explicit factorial and dimension factors.

- **Source locator:** lem:cone-reduction; lem:profile-identities
- **Numinous use:** Carry a polygon and its polar through an affine deformation with a determinant ledger.
- **Limit:** Positive cone duality and a body's polar have different definitions; omitted normalization changes the represented quantity.
